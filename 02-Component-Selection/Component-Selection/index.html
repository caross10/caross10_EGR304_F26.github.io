    Automatic Pet Food Dispenser — Timer/Scheduler Subsystem

    Student: Caleb Ross

    Project: Automatic Pet Food Dispenser

    Subsystem: Timer/Scheduler

    1. Introduction

    The purpose of the Timer/Scheduler subsystem is to generate and condition timing signals for the Automatic Pet Food Dispenser. The subsystem interfaces with the PIC18F57Q43 Curiosity Nano microcontroller and other parts of the dispenser to support the timing and control functions required by the design. The major components considered in this report are a clock/timer integrated circuit, an MCP6004-I/P operational amplifier used in the comparator stage, and a 5 V voltage regulator. In the block diagram, the timer provides an analog timing signal to the comparator stage, which produces a digital output for the PIC18F57Q43. The voltage regulator provides a regulated supply for the subsystem. The microcontroller was preselected for the project and is excluded from the component comparison tables. Pushbuttons, resistors, capacitors, wires, and ordinary PCB materials are also excluded because they are not considered major components under the assignment requirements.

    2. Clock/Timer Selection

    The clock/timer generates the timing waveform used by the Timer/Scheduler subsystem. The candidates were compared based on supply voltage, power consumption, timing flexibility, operating frequency, package type, and ease of implementation.

    Table 1. Clock/Timer Candidates

    Solution Advantages Disadvantages
    Option 1: Texas Instruments LM555CN/NOPB — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $1.44. Stock: 14,800 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - The 8-pin through-hole package is easy to use for breadboard prototyping and PCB assembly.
    - Operates from 4.5 V to 16 V, making it compatible with a 5 V supply.
    - Supports monostable and astable configurations for one-shot timing or repeating oscillation.
    - Widely documented and suitable for basic timing applications.    		 - Typical supply current is higher than that of CMOS timer alternatives.
    - The minimum operating voltage is 4.5 V.
    - Timing accuracy depends on external resistor and capacitor tolerances.
    - Does not independently maintain the time of day or generate a precision analog ramp without additional circuitry.
    Option 2: Texas Instruments TLC555CP — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $1.40. Stock: 13,095 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - Operates from 2 V to 15 V and is compatible with the 5 V supply.
    - CMOS design provides substantially lower supply current than a conventional bipolar 555 timer.
    - Through-hole 8-pin package simplifies prototyping and assembly.
    - Supports monostable and astable operation.
    - Suitable for low-power timing circuits.    		 - Requires external timing components.
    - Timing accuracy depends on the resistor, capacitor, and operating conditions.
    - A digital output does not directly provide a linear analog ramp.
    - Costs slightly more than some basic bipolar timer alternatives.
    Option 3: Texas Instruments NE555P — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $0.59. Stock: 50,761 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - Lower unit cost than the other candidates.
    - Through-hole 8-pin package is convenient for prototyping and soldering.
    - Supports monostable and astable timing configurations.
    - Operates from a supply voltage compatible with a 5 V system.
    - Has manufacturer documentation and is widely used in basic timing circuits.    		 - Higher supply-current consumption than CMOS timer alternatives.
    - Timing accuracy depends on external resistor and capacitor tolerances.
    - The timing interval is affected by the selected external components.
    - Does not independently maintain the time of day or provide a precision analog ramp without additional circuitry.

    Final Clock/Timer Selection

    Choice: Texas Instruments TLC555CP.

    The TLC555CP is the preferred candidate because it provides the timing functions needed for the subsystem while consuming less supply current than conventional bipolar 555 timer alternatives. Its 2 V to 15 V operating range makes it compatible with the planned 5 V supply, and its through-hole package simplifies assembly and prototyping. The LM555CN/NOPB is another suitable candidate, but its higher supply-current consumption makes it less attractive for a low-power embedded system. The NE555P has the lowest unit cost of the three candidates and is a reasonable alternative when minimizing component cost is the main priority. The final timing resistor and capacitor values must be selected according to the required interval. The output waveform must also be verified against the signal-conditioning stage. A 555 timer produces a timing waveform, and its timing-capacitor voltage normally changes between internal threshold levels; it does not automatically produce a linear 0–5 V analog ramp.

    3. Operational Amplifier Selection

    The Operational Amplifier receives the timer’s analog signal and produces a digital output for the PIC18F57Q43. The component must operate from the available supply voltage and provide input and output levels suitable for the intended signal. The candidates were compared based on supply-voltage compatibility, input/output voltage range, current consumption, package type, bandwidth, cost, and ease of assembly.

    Table 2. Operational Amplifier Candidates

    Solution Advantages Disadvantages
    Option 1: Microchip MCP6004-I/P — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $0.59. Stock: 3,544 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - Operates from 1.8 V to 6 V, making it compatible with a 5 V supply.
    - Provides rail-to-rail input and output operation.
    - Contains four operational amplifiers in one package.
    - Through-hole 14-pin package simplifies assembly.
    - Low supply current makes it suitable for low-power signal conditioning.    		 - Four amplifier channels may be more than the subsystem requires.
    - Approximately 1 MHz gain-bandwidth product limits high-frequency performance.
    - Output current is limited by the device's electrical specifications.
    - External feedback components are needed for amplifier configurations.
    Option 2: Texas Instruments LM358P — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $0.33. Stock: 25,138 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - Low unit cost.
    - Two operational amplifiers in an 8-pin through-hole package.
    - Suitable for many low-frequency amplifier and buffer circuits.
    - Supports single-supply operation with a 5 V rail.
    - Widely documented and straightforward to prototype.    		 - Input common-mode range does not extend fully to the positive supply rail.
    - Output is not rail-to-rail.
    - The available output range must be checked against the PIC ADC requirements.
    - Its bandwidth and input/output limitations may restrict its suitability for some signal-conditioning configurations.
    Option 3: Texas Instruments LM324N — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $0.55. Stock: 51,889 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - Four operational amplifiers in a 14-pin through-hole package.
    - Supports single-supply operation with a 5 V rail.
    - Low unit cost.
    - Can support several analog signal-conditioning stages.
    - Through-hole construction is practical for prototyping.    		 - Output is not rail-to-rail.
    - Input common-mode range does not extend fully to the positive supply rail.
    - The output voltage may not reach the upper end of the ADC's input range.
    - Four channels may be unnecessary if only one signal requires conditioning.

    Final Operational Amplifier Selection

    Choice: Microchip MCP6004-I/P.

    The MCP6004-I/P is the preferred candidate because it operates from the planned 5 V supply and provides rail-to-rail input and output capability. These features are useful in the comparator stage shown in the block diagram, where the analog timer signal is converted into a digital signal for the PIC. Its behavior when used as a comparator must still be verified against the manufacturer datasheet. Its low supply current is also beneficial for an embedded system. Although the device contains four amplifiers, the additional channels provide flexibility if more signal-conditioning stages are needed later. The LM358P has a lower unit cost, but its input and output limitations make it less suitable when the signal must use most of the 0–5 V range. The LM324N offers four amplifier channels but does not provide rail-to-rail output operation. The comparator threshold and input signal range must be confirmed during circuit design. The output must satisfy the PIC digital-input voltage specifications. The MCP6004 is an operational amplifier rather than a dedicated comparator, so its suitability for the switching behavior required by this block must be verified using its datasheet.

    4. 5 V Voltage Regulator Selection

    The voltage regulator provides a stable 5 V supply for the subsystem's active electronics. The regulator must supply the required load current while remaining within its electrical and thermal limits. The candidates were compared based on output voltage, rated current, package type, protection features, cost, and thermal considerations.

    Table 3. 5 V Voltage Regulator Candidates

    Solution Advantages Disadvantages
    Option 1: STMicroelectronics L7805CV — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $1.08. Stock: 87,190 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - Provides a fixed regulated 5 V output.
    - Nominal output-current capability of 1.5 A.
    - Through-hole TO-220 package is practical for assembly and heat sinking.
    - Includes overtemperature and short-circuit protection.
    - Low unit cost and established manufacturer documentation.    		 - Linear regulation dissipates excess input power as heat.
    - Requires sufficient input voltage above 5 V.
    - A heatsink may be required at high load current or with a large input-to-output voltage difference.
    - The rated current cannot be assumed to be continuously available under all thermal conditions.
    Option 2: onsemi MC7805CTG — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $0.99. Stock: 43,687 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - Provides a fixed 5 V output.
    - Nominal output-current capability of 1 A.
    - Through-hole TO-220 package is easy to assemble.
    - Includes thermal shutdown and current-limiting protection.
    - Lower unit cost than some alternative regulators.    		 - Its nominal 1 A output rating is lower than the 1.5 A rating of the L7805CV.
    - Linear regulation produces heat as input voltage and output current increase.
    - Requires sufficient input voltage above 5 V.
    - Thermal performance must be checked before selecting the regulator for the complete load.
    Option 3: Texas Instruments LM340AT-5.0/NOPB — DigiKey product page and datasheet.
    Unit price: approximately $1.97. Stock: 13,152 units listed in the retrieved listing.    		 - Provides a fixed regulated 5 V output.
    - Nominal output-current capability of 1.5 A.
    - Through-hole TO-220 package is suitable for assembly and heat sinking.
    - Manufacturer documentation provides electrical and thermal specifications.
    - Includes thermal and current protection features.    		 - Higher unit cost than the L7805CV.
    - Linear-regulator power loss must be managed.
    - Requires sufficient input voltage above 5 V.
    - Full rated output current may require additional thermal management.

    Final Voltage Regulator Selection

    Choice: STMicroelectronics L7805CV.

    The L7805CV is the preferred candidate because it provides a fixed 5 V output, has a nominal 1.5 A output-current rating, uses a through-hole package, and has a lower unit price than the LM340AT-5.0/NOPB. The MC7805CTG has a lower unit cost, but its nominal 1 A current rating provides less capacity if the subsystem's final current requirement increases. The LM340AT-5.0/NOPB provides a similar nominal current rating to the L7805CV but has a higher unit cost. The regulator must be checked against the total system load and the external supply voltage.

    6. Final Component Selection Summary

    Major component Selected candidate Main reason for selection
    Clock/Timer Texas Instruments TLC555CP Low supply current, 5 V compatibility, through hole package, and adequate timing capability.
    Comparator stage / Operational Amplifier Microchip MCP6004-I/P Rail to rail input/output operation, low voltage compatibility, and low supply current.
    5 V Voltage Regulator STMicroelectronics L7805CV Fixed 5 V output, nominal 1.5 A capability, through hole package, and favorable cost and availability.

    7. Conclusion

    The component-selection process compared three commercially available solutions for each major component category. The candidates were evaluated based on electrical specifications, compatibility with the 5 V supply, current consumption, package type, cost, and availability of manufacturer documentation. The TLC555CP is the preferred timer because it offers low supply-current consumption and flexible timing configurations. The MCP6004-I/P is the preferred component for the comparator stage because its rail to rail input and output capability is useful for converting the timer signal into a digital signal for the PIC, subject to verification of its suitability for comparator operation. The L7805CV is the preferred regulator because it provides a fixed 5 V output and a nominal 1.5 A output-current rating in a practical through-hole package. The final implementation must verify the required timing interval, signal waveform, comparator threshold and switching behavior, regulator thermal dissipation, and total power consumption. These checks are necessary to ensure that the selected components work together correctly and meet the electrical requirements of the Automatic Pet Food Dispenser.
